Design review: Checkout (mobile)

Verdict: Needs rework: the main pay button looks disabled and fails contrast, and the form has no labels.

Goal of this screen: A shopper who has chosen the earbuds wants to pay quickly; primary action: Pay ₹2,548.

Fix these first

  1. Make "Pay ₹2,548" the only filled primary button (#2563EB with white text, 5.17:1) and turn PayPal into an outlined secondary button.
  2. Put a visible label above every field and show the phone error as text under the field.
  3. Make the quantity − and + buttons at least 44×44 pt.
  1. 1Blocker"Pay ₹2,548" button

    Problem: Grey text on grey, sampled #9E9E9E on #BDBDBD = 1.43:1 (needs 4.5:1). It looks disabled.

    Why it matters: The main action of the screen is the hardest thing to see; shoppers will wait for it to "enable" or leave.

    Fix: Use the primary style: #2563EB background, #FFFFFF text (5.17:1).

  2. 2Major"Pay with PayPal" button

    Problem: PayPal is the strongest button on the screen, so it competes with, and beats, the main card payment.

    Why it matters: Two primary-looking actions side by side slow the decision and push most people to the wrong one.

    Fix: Make PayPal an outlined secondary button below the main pay button, full width.

  3. 3BlockerShipping address fields

    Problem: Placeholders are the only labels; they vanish when typing and are very light, sampled #C7C7CC on white = 1.68:1.

    Why it matters: Once a field is filled, nobody can check what it was for; screen readers and low-vision users miss the field names (WCAG 3.3.2, 1.4.3).

    Fix: Add a 13px label above each field in #1D1D1F; keep placeholders only for examples like "10-digit mobile number".

  4. 4MajorPhone number field in error

    Problem: Only a red border shows the error; there is no message.

    Why it matters: Users cannot tell what is wrong with "98765", and colour alone fails for colour-blind users (WCAG 1.4.1, 3.3.1).

    Fix: Show "Enter a 10-digit mobile number" under the field with an error icon, and announce it to screen readers.

  5. 5MajorQuantity − and + buttons

    Problem: The buttons are 20×20 pt, below the 24 px web minimum and far below 44 pt.

    Why it matters: Hard to tap on a phone; mis-taps change the order by mistake.

    Fix: Make each button 44×44 pt (the visible box can stay small with a larger tap area).

  6. 6MajorHeader

    Problem: No back or close control, and no way to edit the cart from here.

    Why it matters: Users who want to change their order have to use the browser back button and may lose what they typed.

    Fix: Add a back arrow on the left of the header with an accessible name "Back to cart".

  7. 7MinorOrder summary, Total row

    Problem: Total uses the same size, weight and colour as Subtotal and Delivery.

    Why it matters: The number people check before paying does not stand out.

    Fix: Make the Total row 17px semibold in #1D1D1F, with a divider above it.

  8. 8Minor"Have a promo code?" link

    Problem: The promo code entry comes after the pay buttons, in 12px text.

    Why it matters: Shoppers find it after deciding to pay, and the total they saw changes afterwards.

    Fix: Move it into the order summary card above Total, at 15px.

  9. 9MinorProduct details line

    Problem: 11px text, sampled #B0B0B5 on white = 2.16:1.

    Why it matters: Colour and delivery time are useful to confirm, but hard to read.

    Fix: Use 13px in #6E6E73 (5.07:1).

  10. 10PolishCorner radius

    Problem: Inputs use a 3px radius, cards 14px and buttons a full pill.

    Why it matters: Mixed radii make the screen feel assembled from different kits.

    Fix: Use the card radius family: 10px for inputs, keep pills only for buttons.

Missing states

  • Paying: no loading state for the pay button, so double taps can charge twice.
  • Payment failed: no designed error for a declined card or a cancelled PayPal flow.
  • Invalid PIN code: no message, and no city or state shown to confirm the address.
  • Long product names: no rule for wrapping or truncating the title next to the quantity control.

What works

  • Clear grouping into product, address and summary cards.
  • Prices are right-aligned, so they are easy to scan and compare.
  • One column, top to bottom, which suits a phone.
